## 1. History & Evolution

Drum & Bass emerged from the UK breakbeat hardcore and jungle scenes of the early 1990s. The genre has undergone significant evolution:

| Era | BPM | Dominant Style | Key Tracks / Events |
|--------|-------|----------------|---------------------------|
| 1990–1991 | ~130 BPM | Breakbeat hardcore | Lennie De Ice — "We Are I.E." |
| 1992–1993 | 155–165 BPM | Early jungle | Amen break explosion, pirate radio era |
| 1994–1995 | 160–170 BPM | Jungle / early DnB | Goldie — "Timeless", Metalheadz |
| 1996–1997 | 170–176 BPM | Techstep, intelligent | Roni Size wins Mercury Prize, Prototype Records |
| 1998–2000 | 172–176 BPM | Neurofunk, liquid funk | Ed Rush & Optical — "Wormhole", Hospital Records |
| 2000–2010 | 174–176 BPM | Liquid, neurofunk, jump-up | Pendulum crossover, global expansion |
| 2010–2020 | 172–178 BPM | Deep/minimal, drumstep | Exit Records, Ivy Lab halftime movement |
| 2020–present | 172–178 BPM | Renaissance era | 94% UK streaming growth 2021–2024, Bou, Nia Archives |

## 2. BPM Range & Tempo Map

The DnB tempo spectrum ranges **160–180 BPM**, with **174 BPM** being the most common tempo across all sub-genres.

### Tempo by Sub-genre

| Sub-genre | BPM Range | Typical BPM | Notes |
|---------------|---------------|-----------------|----------|
| Jungle | 160–170 | 165 | Slower, breakbeat-focused |
| Liquid DnB | 170–178 | 174–176 | Often on the faster side |
| Neurofunk | 172–176 | 174 | Tight, precise timing |
| Jump Up | 172–176 | 174 | Dancefloor-oriented |
| Deep / Minimal | 170–175 | 172–174 | Slightly slower, more space |
| Techstep | 170–176 | 173 | Dark, industrial |
| Halftime / 140 | 140–150 | 145 | Drumstep / hip-hop hybrid |
| Drumstep | 140–175 | 140–150 | Half-time drums at DnB tempo |

## 3. Sub-genre Classification

### 3.1 Liquid Drum & Bass (Liquid Funk)

- **BPM:** 170–178 (typically 174–176)
- **Emerged:** Early 2000s, from mid-90s "intelligent jungle"
- **Aesthetic:** Warm, euphoric, soulful — accessible, melodic
- **Key characteristics:**
  - Harmonic/melodic grooves with jazz/funk/soul samples
  - Atmospheric pads, warm basslines, emotional chord progressions
  - Soulful vocals (often R&B or neo-soul style)
  - Rolling beats with less aggressive percussion
  - Rich reverb and delay on melodic elements
  - Softer, more musical basslines (often Reese-based)
- **Pioneers:** High Contrast, Calibre, LTJ Bukem, Fabio, Commix, Nu:Tone, Logistics, London Elektricity
- **Labels:** Hospital Records, Good Looking Records, Soul:R, Creative Source, Fokuz Recordings

### 3.2 Neurofunk

- **BPM:** 172–176 (typically 174)
- **Emerged:** Late 1990s (term coined ~1998), evolved from techstep
- **Aesthetic:** Dark, futuristic, clinical, tech-heavy — designed to induce tension
- **Key characteristics:**
  - Heavily processed Reese basslines with complex modulation
  - FM synthesis and aggressive distortion on bass
  - Tight, precise, often mechanical drum programming
  - Dark sci-fi atmosphere and soundscapes
  - Complex rolling bass patterns — constantly evolving
  - Minimal melodic content; focus on texture and rhythm
  - High production polish — clinical sound
- **Pioneers:** Ed Rush & Optical, Bad Company UK, Noisia, Phace, Misanthrop, Black Sun Empire, Audio, Mefjus
- **Labels:** Virus Recordings, Subtitles Music, Shogun Audio, Eatbrain, Vision Recordings

### 3.3 Jump Up

- **BPM:** 172–176 (typically 174)
- **Emerged:** Mid-1990s
- **Aesthetic:** Fun, aggressive, party-oriented — designed for "bass-face" moments
- **Key characteristics:**
  - Heavy, robotic, often "hoover"-style bass sounds
  - Catchy, repetitive bass hooks that are simple but effective
  - Less serious, often humorous tone compared to other sub-genres
  - Minimal breakdowns — more focus on dancefloor impact
  - Call-and-response bass patterns
  - Loud, aggressive mixdowns
  - Simplified drums to give bass maximum impact
- **Pioneers:** DJ Hype, Hazard, Original Sin, Taxman, Heist, Sub Zero, Annix, Turno
- **Labels:** True Playaz, Ganja Records, Playaz, Dubplate Riddim

### 3.4 Jungle

- **BPM:** 160–170
- **Emerged:** Early 1990s (direct precursor to DnB)
- **Aesthetic:** Warm, raw, organic — heavy Jamaican sound system influence
- **Key characteristics:**
  - Chopped breakbeats (Amen, Think, Funky Drummer) — the defining element
  - Heavy sub-bass, often reggae/dub-influenced
  - Ragga/dancehall vocal samples, MC chants
  - Sound system culture — built for big rigs
  - Dub/reggae influence (echo, reverb, delay)
  - Lo-fi, sampled aesthetic — less polished than modern DnB
  - "Amen break" as foundational rhythmic element
- **Pioneers:** Goldie, LTJ Bukem, 4hero, Doc Scott, Grooverider, Shy FX, DJ Hype, Ray Keith
- **Labels:** Metalheadz, Moving Shadow, V Recordings, Suburban Base, Reinforced Records

### 3.5 Deep / Minimal DnB

- **BPM:** 170–175 (typically 172–174)
- **Emerged:** 2010s
- **Aesthetic:** Rollered, hypnotic, space-conscious — heads-down, late-night vibe
- **Key characteristics:**
  - Sparse arrangements — "less is more" philosophy
  - Rolling sub-bass lines (often 808-style)
  - Stripped-back drums with minimal percussion layers
  - Emphasis on space, groove, and restraint
  - Extended builds with gradual evolution
  - Subtle sound design — small details matter
  - Dark, minimal aesthetic — no melodic clutter
- **Pioneers:** Skeptical, dBridge (Exit Records), Synkro, Loxy, Consequence, Sabre, Alix Perez, Ivy Lab (early)
- **Labels:** Exit Records, 31 Records, Dispatch Recordings, Ingredients, Samurai Music

### 3.6 Techstep

- **BPM:** 170–176
- **Emerged:** Mid-1990s
- **Aesthetic:** Industrial, cold, futuristic, dystopian
- **Key characteristics:**
  - Sci-fi soundscapes and dystopian samples
  - Industrial/techno influence in percussion
  - Minimalist approach — stripped-back compared to jungle
  - Heavy distortion and reverb
  - Metallic basslines with aggressive processing
  - Dark, moody atmosphere throughout
  - Direct precursor to neurofunk
- **Pioneers:** Ed Rush, Trace, Nico, Fierce, Dom & Roland, Optical, Bad Company UK (early)
- **Labels:** Moving Shadow, Prototype Recordings, Quarantine, Renegade Hardware, No U-Turn

### 3.7 Additional Sub-genres

| Sub-genre | BPM | Characteristics |
|----------------|--------|---------------------|
| Darkstep | 170–180 | Fast drums, dark/industrial/hardcore influences |
| Hardstep | 170–175 | Gritty basslines, simple heavy melodies |
| Jazzstep | 170–180 | Jazz scales/instrumentation, trip-hop crossover |
| Ragga Jungle | 160–170 | Dancehall/reggae vocals, jungle breaks |
| Drill 'n' Bass | 160–180 | Complex irregular rhythms, IDM (Squarepusher, Aphex Twin) |
| Sambass | 170–175 | Brazilian samba/bossa nova fusion (DJ Marky) |
| Crossbreed | 170–175+ | DnB + industrial hardcore/gabber/metal |
| Atmospheric / Intelligent | 170–178 | Ambient pads, cinematic, world music influences |
| Metal & Bass | 170–175 | Heavy metal guitar + DnB (Pendulum, The Qemists) |

## 4. Arrangement Structure

### 4.1 Standard DnB Arrangement

The typical DnB track follows this arrangement structure (at 174 BPM, counted in bars of 4/4):

```
Section       | Bars  | Time    | Description
--------------|-------|---------|-------------------------------
Intro         | 16–32 | 0:22–0:44 | Sparse percussion, atmosphere, bassline hint
First Drop    | 16–32 | 0:22–0:44 | Full drums, sub bass, main hook
Breakdown     | 16–32 | 0:22–0:44 | Reduced elements, pads, filtered
Build         | 8–16  | 0:11–0:22 | Risers, snare rolls, filter sweeps
Second Drop   | 32    | 0:44     | More intense, extra layers, variations
Bridge / Mini | 8–16  | 0:11–0:22 | Brief reset before final section
Third Drop    | 16–24 | 0:22–0:33 | Final energy peak
Outro         | 8–16  | 0:11–0:22 | Strip back, fade out
```

**Total track length:** ~128–160 bars (~3:00–5:00 minutes)

### 4.2 Detailed Section Breakdown

#### Intro (16–32 bars)
- Sparse percussion — often hats, shakers, and ride cymbal only
- Atmospheric pads or field recordings
- Bassline hint (filtered, low-passed, or side-chained)
- Kick on 4/4 at reduced volume initially
- Sub bass often introduced at bar 8
- Full kick pattern typically enters at bar 16
- No snare or minimal snare (ghost hits only)

#### Build / First Drop (16–32 bars)
- Full drum pattern established
- Main bassline hook introduced
- Arrangement marker — feels like "the track starts here"
- Often a 16-bar loop repeated exactly, then varied on second repeat
- Key elements: sub bass, main bass, full drums, atmospheric pads
- Extra percussion layers added toward the end (16-bar mark)

#### Breakdown (16–32 bars)
- Drums reduced — often just hats or ride
- Pads and atmosphere take center stage
- Main bass drops out or becomes filtered
- Melodic elements (piano, strings, vocals) featured here
- Reverb/delay throws on snare or other percussive hits
- Half-time feel common (snare on beat 1 instead of 2 & 4)
- Used as emotional reset before next drop

#### Build-up (8–16 bars)
- Snare roll starting at 8th notes, increasing to 16ths, then 32nds
- Filter sweep on bass/pads (low-pass opening up to high-pass)
- Risers (white noise sweep + pitch riser)
- Kick density increases (last 4 bars: every beat)
- Reverb send increases on snare (snare getting "bigger")
- Classic "Amen snare roll" pattern: last 2 bars of build

#### Second Drop (32 bars)
- Often the most intense section of the track
- Extra bass layers or bass variation
- Drum fills and variations more frequent
- New melodic elements or variations introduced
- Often 32 bars (vs. 16–24 for first drop)
- Greater energy than first drop

#### Mini-break / Bridge (8–16 bars)
- Brief reset section (optional)
- Used before third drop in longer tracks
- Often just 8 bars of filtered drums + bass
- Functions as a 'rest' before final energy

#### Third Drop / Alternative Drop (16–24 bars)
- May switch to half-time feel for variation
- Can be a "bass switch" — entirely different bass pattern
- Final energy peak before outro
- Often shorter than main drops

#### Outro (8–16 bars)
- Gradual removal of elements
- Drums stripped back first
- Reverb tails ring out
- Last 4 bars: bass out, drums minimal
- Final hit or reverse cymbal as end marker

### 4.3 Arrangement Variations by Sub-genre

**Liquid DnB:**
- Longer breakdowns (24–32 bars) — more focus on melody
- Intro often 32 bars with extended atmosphere
- Vocals drive arrangement — phrases determine section changes
- Build-ups use orchestral risers and vocal sweeps
- Second drop may feature new melody rather than bass variation

**Neurofunk:**
- Shorter intros (16 bars) — straight to the action
- Breakdowns often 16 bars (or less) — minimal reset time
- Build-ups use snare rolls + glitched risers (noise + pitch modulation)
- Third drop common (track goes through multiple "bass ideas")
- Asymmetrical arrangement (e.g., 24-bar first drop, 16-bar breakdown)
- More frequent mini-breaks between sections

**Jump Up:**
- Minimal breakdowns (8–16 bars) — get back to the drop ASAP
- Intro often 16 bars — enough for a DJ mix
- Very few atmospheric elements — focus on bass hooks
- Builds use "bass face" risers (filtered bass building)
- Often has three drops with slight bass variations
- Outro can be as short as 4 bars (DJ tool style)

**Deep / Minimal:**
- Very long intros (32–64 bars) — extended atmosphere
- Breakdowns subtle — don't fully clear out
- Builds are gradual — slow filter opens over 16 bars
- Drops less dramatic — more of a "roll" into the section
- Outro can be 16–32 bars

## 5. Drum Patterns & Breakbeats

### 5.1 The Amen Break — Foundation of DnB

- **Origin:** The Winstons — "Amen, Brother" (1969), drum break by Gregory Coleman
- **Original tempo:** ~162 BPM
- **Length:** 4 bars (one loop)
- **DnB usage:** Pitched up to 170–172 BPM, or time-stretched
- **Significance:** Most sampled break in electronic music history

**Amen Break structure (original):**

```
Count:  1 e + a  2 e + a  3 e + a  4 e + a
Kick:   X . . .  . . . .  X . . .  . . . .
Snare:  . . . .  . X . .  . . . .  X . . .
Hat:    . X . X  . X . X  . X . X  . X . X
Ghost:  . . X .  . . X .  . . . .  . . . .
Ride:   . . . .  . . . .  . . . .  . . . .
```

**How the Amen is chopped in DnB:**
1. Slice into individual hits (8–32 slices per bar)
2. Map slices to MIDI pads / sampler
3. Re-arrange into 2-step grid pattern
4. Layer original break under programmed hits for texture
5. Pitch individual slices up/down for variation
6. Add swing (60–70% swing on 16th-note ghost hits)

### 5.2 Other Classic Breaks

| Break | Origin | BPM | Character |
|-----------|------------|---------|---------------|
| Think Break | Lyn Collins — "Think (About It)" | ~163 | Tight, clean, great for chopping |
| Funky Drummer | James Brown | ~156 | Loose, swung feel, complex hi-hats |
| Apache | Incredible Bongo Band | ~165 | Bongo-heavy, tribal feel |
| Hot Pants | Bobby Byrd / James Brown | ~170 | Punchy kick, tight snare |
| Tramen | Hybrid break (Think + Amen layered) | ~170+ | Common in modern neurofunk |
| Soul Pride | James Brown | ~160 | Deep, punchy kick |

### 5.3 The Classic 2-Step DnB Drum Grid

The **2-step** pattern is the standard DnB drum grid. It's called "2-step" because the kick typically hits twice per bar, and the snare hits on beats 2 and 4.

```
Kick:  X . . .  . . . .  X . . .  . . . .
Snare: . . . .  X . . .  . . . .  X . . .
HiHat: X X X X  X X X X  X X X X  X X X X   (8th notes)
  or:  X . X .  X . X .  X . X .  X . X .   (8th notes)
  or:  L R L R  L R L R  L R L R  L R L R   (16th note roll - "rolling hats")
```

**Ghost note patterns (16th note offbeats):**

```
Ghost: . X . .  X . . X  . X . .  X . . X
Rim:   . . X .  . . . .  . . X .  . . . .
```

**Standard velocity map (MIDI):**
- Kick: 100–127 (accents on 1 and 3)
- Snare: 100–127 (accents on 2 and 4)
- Hi-hat: 70–90 (main hits), 40–60 (ghosts), 100–110 (accents)
- Ghost snare: 30–50
- Ride/crash: 80–110

### 5.4 Snare Rolls & Build Techniques

**Standard snare roll patterns (for build-ups):**

```
8th notes:  X . X . X . X .
16th notes: X.X.X.X.X.X.X.X.
32nd notes: XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX
Triplets:   X..X..X..X..X..
```

**Fills:**
- **The "Amen" fill:** Last beat of phrase — kick-kick-snare on 16th triplets
- **The "Phaze" fill:** 32nd note snare + tom roll → crash on next downbeat
- **The "timestretch" fill:** Snare hit with extreme reverb tail, stretched over 1–2 bars

### 5.5 Drum Processing Chain

**Typical DnB drum bus chain:**

1. **EQ:**
   - Low cut at 40 Hz (sub rumble removal)
   - High shelf +2 dB above 10 kHz (air)
   - Cut 2–3 dB at 250–400 Hz (mud removal)

2. **Compression:**
   - Parallel compression (New York style)
   - Blend: 20–40% wet
   - Fast attack (0.5–2 ms), medium release (50–150 ms)
   - Ratio: 4:1 to 8:1

3. **Transient shaping:**
   - Kick: increase attack 2–5 ms, sustain -2 dB
   - Snare: increase attack 1–3 ms, sustain +1 dB
   - Hats: soften attack, boost sustain for roll effect

4. **Saturation:**
   - Tape saturation (warmth, harmonics)
   - Soft clipping on drum bus (3–6 dB of reduction)

5. **Reverb:**
   - Short room reverb on snare (0.3–0.6s decay, 10–15 ms predelay)
   - Separate verb send for "reverb throws"

### 5.6 Drum Layering Techniques

- **Kick layering:**
  - Sub layer (60–80 Hz) + punch layer (100–150 Hz) + click layer (2–5 kHz)
  - Sub layer: 808 style sine kick
  - Punch layer: acoustic kick with fast attack
  - Click layer: short noise burst with pitch envelope

- **Snare layering:**
  - Body layer (200–400 Hz) + snap layer (5–8 kHz) + low layer (150–250 Hz)
  - Body: acoustic snare or clap
  - Snap: white noise burst (20–40 ms)
  - Low: low-tuned tom or 808 snare for weight

## 6. Bass Sound Design

### 6.1 Sub Bass

The sub bass sits at **40–80 Hz** and is the foundation of the DnB low-end.

**Design:**
- **Waveform:** Pure sine wave (sometimes with subtle saturation)
- **Processing:**
  - Minimal — keep it clean
  - Optional: subtle saturation (Decapitator, Saturn) at 5–10% mix
  - Sidechain compression to kick (release: 150–250 ms)
- **Level:** -6 to -3 dB relative to kick

**Frequency mapping (key of C):**
| Note | Frequency | Note | Frequency |
|----------|---------------|----------|---------------|
| C2 | 65.4 Hz | C1 | 32.7 Hz |
| D2 | 73.4 Hz | D1 | 36.7 Hz |
| E2 | 82.4 Hz | E1 | 41.2 Hz |
| F2 | 87.3 Hz | F1 | 43.7 Hz |
| G2 | 98.0 Hz | G1 | 49.0 Hz |
| A2 | 110.0 Hz | A1 | 55.0 Hz |
| B2 | 123.5 Hz | B1 | 61.7 Hz |

### 6.2 Reese Bass

The Reese is the classic DnB bass sound — rich, wide, and detuned.

**Synthesis (Serum / Massive / Vital):**
- **Oscillators:** 2–4 sawtooth oscillators
- **Detune:** ±5–20 cents per oscillator
- **Spread:** 50–80% stereo spread on oscillators
- **Filter:** Low-pass 24dB/oct at ~500 Hz (automated)
- **Filter envelope:** Cutoff opens to 2–3 kHz during drop

**Processing chain:**
1. EQ: high-pass at 80 Hz, boost 2–4 dB at 800–1500 Hz
2. Distortion: Tube/overdrive saturation (Softube Saturation Knob, Decapitator)
3. Multiband compression: Split at 200 Hz and 2 kHz
   - Sub band (below 200 Hz): slight compression (2:1)
   - Mid band (200–2k Hz): heavy compression (4:1) for aggression
4. Stereo processing:
   - Chorus/flanger for width
   - Mid-side EQ: mono below 200 Hz, stereo above
5. Filter automation: LP cutoff follows arrangement (opens on drops)

### 6.3 Neuro / Growl Bass

The neurofunk bass sound uses FM synthesis for complex, aggressive timbres.

**FM Synthesis (FM8 / Operator / Serum FM):**
- **FM algorithm:** Carrier(s) modulated by 1–3 modulators
- **Common ratios:** 1:1, 2:1, 2:3, 3:4 (modulator:carrier)
- **Modulation index:** 2–5 (controlled via macro)
- **Feedback:** 20–40% for added grit

**Key modulation targets:**
- Pitch envelope: Fast decay (10–30 ms) on modulator pitch for "growl"
- Filter cutoff: Envelope follow + LFO (1/4 note rate)
- FM amount: LFO syncopated to rhythm
- Waveform: Cycle through sin→tri→saw via morph

**Typical neurofunk bass chain:**
1. Band-pass filter: 800–1500 Hz focus
2. Distortion: Hard clip / decimate / bitcrush
   - Waveshaper: threshold -12 dB, hard clip curve
3. EQ: Low cut at 100 Hz, high cut at 5 kHz
4. Reverb: Small room, 20% wet, 0.1–0.3s decay
5. Compression: Aggressive (4:1 to 10:1 ratio)
6. Layering: Growl mid (300 Hz–3 kHz) + Reese pad (wide) + Sub (sine)

### 6.4 Jump Up Bass

Jump Up bass is characterized by aggressive, often "hoover" or "raspy" sounds.

- **Waveform:** Square/saw with hard sync, FM
- **Distortion:** Heavy — fuzz, overdrive, waveshaping
- **Filter:** Band-pass with envelope and LFO modulation
- **Unique feature:** Often uses glissando/portamento for "sliding" notes
- **Pattern:** Call-and-response, rhythmic, syncopated 8th/16th notes
- **Processing:** Heavy limiting, often the loudest element in the mix

### 6.5 Bass Arrangement Patterns

**Standard DnB bass pattern:**

- Kick on 1 and 3 → bass plays on 2 and 4 (off-beat)
- Rhythmic syncopation: bass notes often start on 2e, 2+a, 4e
- Sub bass plays root notes on downbeats
- Mid/high bass plays syncopated, rhythmic lines

**Common bass note grids (16th notes):**

```
1 b b b  2 b b b  3 b b b  4 b b b
K . . .  . . . .  K . . .  . . . .   (kick)
. . . .  B . . .  . . . .  B . . .   (reese - on 2 and 4)
. . . .  . . B B  . . . .  . . B .   (growl - syncopated 16ths)
```

## 7. Mix Characteristics & Sound Design

### 7.1 Frequency Distribution — The DnB Frequency Map

DnB mixes are characterized by aggressive low-end, clear mids, and sparkling highs.

```
Frequency Range    | Elements                   | Level (relative)
--------------------|----------------------------|--------------------
20–60 Hz           | Sub bass fundamental       | -6 dB (to kick)
60–100 Hz          | Kick fundamental, sub       | -3 dB (kick peak)
100–250 Hz         | Kick body, bass body, snare body | 0 dB (reference)
250–500 Hz         | Bass harmonics, snare body  | Cut 2–3 dB (mud)
500–800 Hz         | Snare, bass presence        | Neutral / slight cut
800 Hz–2 kHz       | Vocals, pads, mids          | The "clarity pocket"
2–5 kHz            | Hi-hat, snare snap, cymbals | Presence zone
5–8 kHz            | Hi-hat sizzle, crash top    | Boost shelf +2 dB
8–20 kHz           | Air, reverb tails, top end  | Soft high shelf +2 dB
```

### 7.2 Sidechain Compression

Sidechaining is critical in DnB to create the rhythmic "pumping" feel and prevent frequency masking.

**Kick → Sub bass:**
- Compressor on bass channel, sidechain from kick
- Attack: 0.5–2 ms
- Release: 100–250 ms
- Ratio: 4:1 to 10:1 (aggressive)
- Threshold: -20 to -12 dB
- Result: Bass ducks exactly on kick, recovers before next kick

**Kick → Pad / Atmosphere:**
- Attack: 1–5 ms
- Release: 200–400 ms (longer for atmospheric feel)
- Ratio: 2:1 to 4:1 (gentler than bass sidechain)

**Advanced sidechain techniques:**
- Multiband sidechain: compress only below 200 Hz
- Volume shaper (LFO Tool, ShaperBox): draw custom ducking curves
- Sidechain to both kick AND snare for groove
- Ghost kick sidechain: trigger ducking with a muted kick track

### 7.3 Overall Mix Balance (Reference Levels)

| Element | Peak Level | Notes |
|-------------|----------------|-----------|
| Kick | -6 dB | Main reference point |
| Snare | -6 dB | Matched to kick level |
| Sub bass | -8 dB | Below kick |
| Reese / growl | -10 dB | Mid-bass layer |
| Hi-hats | -14 dB | Sits under the beat |
| Crashes / rides | -10 dB | Accents |
| Pads / atmos | -16 dB | Background |
| Vocals | -8 dB | Front of mix |
| FX / risers | -12 dB | Dynamic |

### 7.4 Mastering Chain

**Typical DnB mastering targets:**
- **RMS:** -12 to -8 dB RMS (very loud)
- **LUFS (integrated):** -6 to -4 LUFS
- **True peak:** -1 dB (prevent intersample clipping)
- **Headroom:** Mix peaks at -6 dB before mastering limiter

**Standard mastering chain:**
1. **EQ (subtractive):**
   - Low cut at 30 Hz (butterworth, 12dB/oct)
   - Gentle cut 1–2 dB at 250 Hz
   - High shelf +2 dB above 10 kHz

2. **Multiband compression:**
   - Crossover: 200 Hz, 2 kHz
   - Low band (below 200 Hz): ratio 1.5:1, slow attack
   - Mid band (200–2k Hz): ratio 2:1, medium attack
   - High band (above 2k Hz): ratio 1.3:1, fast attack

3. **Limiting:**
   - Ceiling: -1 dB
   - Input gain: to achieve target LUFS (-6 to -4)
   - Lookahead: 2–5 ms
   - Release: auto or medium (100 ms)

4. **Stereo imaging:**
   - Mono below 200 Hz
   - Mid-side EQ: widen above 2 kHz (+0.5 to +1 dB sides)
   - Correlation meter: aim for 0.3–0.7

## 8. Vocal Processing

### 8.1 Standard DnB Vocal Chain

1. **EQ:**
   - High-pass at 80–120 Hz (remove rumble)
   - Low shelf -2 dB at 300 Hz (reduce mud)
   - Boost 2–4 dB at 2–5 kHz (presence)
   - Air boost +2 dB at 12 kHz

2. **Compression:**
   - Ratio: 3:1 to 4:1
   - Attack: 10–30 ms (let transients through)
   - Release: 50–100 ms
   - Makeup: to hit -8 dB peak

3. **De-essing:**
   - Threshold: -20 dB
   - Reduction: 4–8 dB at 5–8 kHz
   - Lookahead: 1 ms

4. **Reverb:**
   - Hall or plate reverb
   - Decay: 1.5–2.5s
   - Predelay: 30–60 ms
   - Wet/dry: 20–30%
   - EQ reverb return: high-pass at 300 Hz, low-pass at 8 kHz

5. **Delay:**
   - Ping-pong delay, 1/4 or 1/8 note
   - Feedback: 15–30%
   - Filter on repeats: high-pass at 500 Hz, low-pass at 5 kHz

### 8.2 DnB-specific Vocal Techniques

- **Pitch shifting:** +2 to +5 semitones for ethereal "chipmunk" effect
- **Time stretching:** Stretch vocal phrases to match bar grid
- **Stutter edits:** Cut vocal into 1/8 or 1/16 slices, rearrange rhythmically
- **Reverb throws:** Send vocal to 100% wet reverb on phrase endings
- **Vocoder / talkbox:** Used in some liquid tracks for color
- **Granular synthesis:** Grainize vocal for textural pads

## 9. Reverb, Atmosphere & Space

### 9.1 Reverb Settings by Element

| Element | Reverb Type | Decay | Predelay | Mix | Filtering |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------|--------------|---------|---------------|
| Snare | Room / Plate | 0.3–0.8s | 10–20 ms | 30–50% | HP 200 Hz, LP 8 kHz |
| Bass | Room (small) | 0.1–0.3s | 5 ms | 10–20% | HP 300 Hz, LP 5 kHz |
| Pads | Hall | 2–4s | 30–50 ms | 40–60% | HP 300 Hz |
| Vocals | Hall / Plate | 1.5–2.5s | 30–60 ms | 20–30% | HP 300 Hz, LP 8 kHz |
| FX (risers) | Huge hall / Reverse | 4–8s | 50 ms | 60–80% | HP 200 Hz, LP 12 kHz |

### 9.2 Creating Space in DnB

- **Stereo placement:** Keep kick, snare, and sub bass centered (mono); spread hats, rides, pads, and FX wide
- **Reverb return EQ:** Always high-pass reverb returns at 200–300 Hz to avoid mud
- **Automation:** Reverb wash increases on breakdowns, tightens on drops
- **Reverb throws:** Duck main signal when reverb throw hits (sidechain reverb return)
- **Gated reverb:** Classic 80s-style on snares for breakdown drama

### 9.3 Atmosphere / Soundscape Design

- **Field recordings:** Rain, city ambience, machinery — used as background texture
- **Granular synthesis:** Process field recordings into evolving pads
- **Reverse reverb:** Preverb on snare/bass hits for tension
- **White noise sweeps:** High-passed noise (12 kHz+) for transitions
- **Sub drops:** 808-style descending tone for epic transitional moments

## Appendix: Key Terms Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|----------|---------------|
| **Amen Break** | 4-bar drum break from "Amen, Brother" — most sampled break in DnB |
| **2-step** | Standard DnB drum pattern: kick on 1 & 3, snare on 2 & 4 |
| **Reese** | Detuned sawtooth bass pad — named after Terminator track "Reese" by AFX |
| **Roller** | Track with continuous, hypnotic groove — minimal breakdowns |
| **Drop** | The moment full drums + bass enter after a build |
| **Bass-face** | Moment of recognition of a particularly aggressive bass sound |
| **Amen rinse-out** | Extended use of the Amen break as the main rhythmic element |
| **Half-time** | Drum pattern at half the perceived tempo of the track |
| **Jump-up** | Sub-genre focused on bouncy, aggressive bass hooks |
| **Liquid** | Melodic, soulful sub-genre with jazz/funk influences |
| **Neurofunk** | Dark, technical sub-genre with FM bass and complex sound design |
| **Techstep** | Industrial, minimalist precursor to neurofunk |
| **Phaze** | Track by Bad Company UK — iconic for its amen roll |
| **Breaks** | Sampled drum breaks (Amen, Think, Tramen, etc.) |
| **Stab** | Short, percussive chord or note — common in bass patterns |
| **Ride** | Basic cymbal pattern often used as the first element in intro |
